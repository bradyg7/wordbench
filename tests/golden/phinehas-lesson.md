# Phinehas: The Whole Record

## Twenty-five mentions, three men

The name Phinehas appears 25 times in 24 verses of the King James Bible, in eight books: Exodus, Numbers, Joshua, Judges, 1 Samuel, 1 Chronicles, Ezra and Psalms. It never appears in the New Testament.

Three men carry it. Seventeen mentions belong to the son of Eleazar, the grandson of Aaron. Seven belong to a son of Eli, the priest at Shiloh. One, in Ezra 8:33, names the father of an Eleazar who helped weigh the silver and gold after the return from Babylon. The text says nothing more about him.

@Ezra 8:33

All 24 verses that name him are shown in this lesson.

&H6372

The lexicons do not agree about the name. Strong's derives it, "apparently", from the words for mouth and serpent. Brown-Driver-Briggs points to an Egyptian name, Pe-nehasi. Scripture never explains it, so this study stays with what the verses say.

## His life in seven scenes

Exodus 6: he is named in the family of Levi before the Exodus begins.

Numbers 25: at Shittim he rises from the weeping congregation, and the plague stops.

Numbers 31: he goes to the war against Midian with the trumpets in his hand.

Joshua 22: he leads ten princes to ask the eastern tribes about their altar, and a war is called off.

Joshua 24: his father Eleazar is buried on a hill given to Phinehas.

Judges 20: he stands before the ark and asks whether Israel should fight Benjamin again.

1 Chronicles 9: he is remembered as ruler of the keepers of the entry, and the LORD was with him.

## Born into the list

@Exodus 6:23-25

The first mention comes before the Exodus has begun. Exodus 6 stops the story of Moses and Aaron to list the families of Levi, and the list closes with Phinehas. He is the only grandson of Aaron it names. His mother is not named. She is "one of the daughters of Putiel", and Putiel appears nowhere else.

The verse just before him lists the sons of Korah. That family comes back twice in this study.

After Exodus 6:25 the name is silent until Numbers 25, when Israel is camped at Shittim.

## Everyone saw. One man rose.

@Numbers 25:1-3

@Numbers 25:4-6

Before Phinehas appears, the LORD gives Moses an order about the heads of the people, and Moses tells the judges of Israel to slay every man joined to Baal-peor. The chapter does not record any judge carrying it out.

Then a man of Israel brings a Midianitish woman to his brethren "in the sight of Moses, and in the sight of all the congregation", while the people are weeping before the door of the tabernacle. Everyone sees it.

@Numbers 25:7-9

Phinehas sees it too, and three verbs follow: he rose up, he took a javelin, he went in after the man. The KJV renders this Hebrew word "javelin" only here. Its other fourteen uses are spear or spears, a buckler, and lancets.

Two more words in verse 8 occur nowhere else in the Bible: the "tent" and the "belly". Strong's derives both from one root.

Notice when the plague is mentioned. Nothing in verses 1 to 7 says a plague has begun. The reader learns of it in the sentence that says it was stayed, and only then hears its cost: twenty and four thousand.

The names wait until after the LORD has spoken. The man was Zimri, a prince of a chief house among the Simeonites. The woman was Cozbi, the daughter of Zur, head over a people in Midian.

@Numbers 25:14-15

## What the LORD said

@Numbers 25:11
@Numbers 25:11 ylt

The KJV says "zealous for my sake" and "in my jealousy". The Hebrew uses one root three times: a verb, and the same noun twice. Young's Literal keeps it: "zealous with My zeal" and "in My zeal". Both times the noun carries the word my. The zeal is called the LORD's own.

&H7065

&H7068

The verb is rendered "zealous" in only two verses of the whole KJV, Numbers 25:11 and 25:13. Inside Numbers the root appears in three places only: the law of jealousy in chapter 5, Moses asking Joshua "Enviest thou for my sake?" in chapter 11, and here.

The first result named is that he "turned my wrath away". The same two Hebrew words, turn and wrath, are said of Moses in Psalm 106:23, who "stood before him in the breach, to turn away his wrath".

@Numbers 25:12-13

Two things are given: "my covenant of peace", and "the covenant of an everlasting priesthood". The KJV has the phrase "everlasting priesthood" twice, here and in Exodus 40:15, where it was already spoken over Aaron's sons. "Covenant of peace" occurs three times: here, and twice in Ezekiel (34:25 and 37:26).

The reason is given in so many words: "because he was zealous for his God, and made an atonement for the children of Israel". In the KJV, atonement is mostly the language of the altar. Leviticus alone holds 49 of the verb's 102 uses. In Numbers 25 no altar, incense or offering is named.

@Exodus 40:15

The next chapter is dated from this event. "After the plague", the LORD orders a new count of Israel.

## The grandfather's plague

Phinehas is not the first in his family to stop a plague. In Numbers 16, after Korah's rebellion, wrath went out from the LORD and a plague began. Moses sent Aaron into the congregation with a censer.

@Numbers 16:47-48

"He stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stayed." The KJV phrase "the plague was stayed" occurs in five verses: twice of Aaron (Numbers 16:48 and 16:50), once of Phinehas (Numbers 25:8), once at David's altar (2 Samuel 24:25), and once in Psalm 106:30. In Hebrew, Numbers 16:48 and Psalm 106:30 share three words: he stood, it was restrained, the plague.

The differences are as plain as the likeness. Aaron took incense, as Moses commanded, and ran. No command is recorded for Phinehas. He took a javelin. Both are said to have made an atonement.

## Psalm 106 remembers

@Psalm 106:28-29

@Psalm 106:30-31
@Psalm 106:30-31 ylt

The psalm tells the story in four lines. Where Numbers says he took a javelin, the psalm says he "executed judgment". The Hebrew verb behind that phrase is rendered pray, prayed, praying or prayeth 74 times out of 84 in the KJV, and judgment only here. The study leaves that question open.

Then: "that was counted unto him for righteousness unto all generations for evermore". The Hebrew words counted and righteousness stand within six words of each other in only two verses: here, and Genesis 15:6, of Abraham.

@Genesis 15:6

Abraham "believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness". For Phinehas, what was counted is what he did at Peor. Scripture uses the same words for both and does not comment on the pairing, and neither will this lesson.

The psalm's own words connect too. In verse 23 Moses stood in the "breach", and in verse 29 the plague "brake in". Strong's gives one root for both. In verse 16 the verb of Phinehas's zeal is rendered "envied", of those who envied Moses and Aaron.

@Psalm 106:23

## Trumpets in his hand

@Numbers 31:6

In the war against Midian, Moses sends Phinehas, not Eleazar his father, with the holy instruments and "the trumpets to blow in his hand". Eleazar stays, and goes out to meet the army "without the camp" (31:13). The text does not say why.

"In his hand" is the same Hebrew as the javelin "in his hand" in Numbers 25:7. The law had already put the trumpets in the priests' hands for war.

@Numbers 10:8-9

@Numbers 31:8

Among the five kings of Midian slain is a Zur. Cozbi was "the daughter of Zur". Balaam is slain too, and only now does the text say why he matters: the women of Midian acted "through the counsel of Balaam" in the matter of Peor. Numbers 25 never mentions him.

@Numbers 31:15-16

The New Testament remembers Balaam three times (2 Peter 2:15, Jude 11, Revelation 2:14). Revelation names the same two things Numbers 25 begins with: eating what was sacrificed to idols, and fornication.

@Revelation 2:14

## Sent to ask first

@Joshua 22:11-14

When the eastern tribes build a great altar by Jordan, the whole congregation gathers at Shiloh "to go up to war against them". Before any battle, they send Phinehas with ten princes.

@Joshua 22:16-17

The delegation names Peor: "Is the iniquity of Peor too little for us, from which we are not cleansed until this day". The phrase "the iniquity of Peor" occurs only here. The man who stopped that plague now leads the men who recall it.

The word for "trespass" here is the word Numbers 31:16 used of Peor. It runs through this chapter four times, and later through Ezra 9.

Then the eastern tribes answer, for nine verses (21 to 29), and the record shows no interruption.

@Joshua 22:30-31

"It pleased them." These are the first words the record gives to Phinehas himself: "This day we perceive that the LORD is among us". No one dies. The congregation blesses God and goes no further against them.

@Joshua 22:32-33

## A hill in Ephraim

@Joshua 24:33

The book of Joshua ends with the burial of Eleazar "in a hill that pertained to Phinehas his son". In Hebrew the hill is Gibeah, the Gibeah of Phinehas. It lay in mount Ephraim, though the priests' thirteen cities had come by lot from Judah, Simeon and Benjamin (Joshua 21:4). The text does not explain the gift.

## Still standing

@Judges 20:26-28

Phinehas appears one last time, before the ark during the war with Benjamin. Israel had lost twenty-two thousand men on the first day and eighteen thousand on the second (20:21 and 20:25). The question put to the LORD changes the third time: "or shall I cease?" Only the third answer carries a promise: "to morrow I will deliver them into thine hand".

The verb for "stood" is the one used of Aaron between the dead and the living, of Moses in the breach, and of Phinehas in Psalm 106:30. The words "in those days" are all the text gives for when this happened.

This is the last scene in which the son of Eleazar acts. Scripture records the death of Eleazar, and the death of the other Phinehas four times over. It never records his.

## Keeper of the entry

@1 Chronicles 9:19-20

Chronicles adds one line of history: Phinehas "was the ruler over them in time past, and the LORD was with him". Those under him are Korahites, "keepers of the entry". The family of Korah, whose rebellion came before Aaron's plague in Numbers 16, kept the door under Phinehas. The door is where Numbers 25 began.

In the Old Testament of the KJV the words "the LORD was with him" are said of Joseph, Samuel, David, Hezekiah, and Phinehas.

## The line that went on

@1 Chronicles 6:4-8; 1 Chronicles 6:50-53

The priestly line runs Eleazar, Phinehas, Abishua, and on to Zadok in the days of David, to the Azariah who served in the temple Solomon built (6:10), and to Jehozadak, who went into captivity (6:15).

@Ezra 7:1-5

Ezra's own genealogy runs back through Phinehas to "Aaron the chief priest". It is six names shorter than 1 Chronicles 6. Between Azariah and Meraioth it passes over Johanan, Azariah, Ahimaaz, Zadok, Ahitub and Amariah.

@Ezra 8:1-2

Among those who came up with Ezra, the sons of Phinehas stand first, beside the sons of Ithamar.

@Ezra 9:1-2

Then Ezra hears that priests and Levites have taken daughters of the peoples of the lands, Moabites among them, and the "holy seed" is mixed. The word for "trespass" is the one Numbers 31:16 used of Peor. A descendant of Phinehas faces a report like the one at Shittim, where Israel went after the daughters of Moab.

@Nehemiah 13:28-29

Nehemiah chases away a son of the high priest's house married into Sanballat's family, and asks God to remember those who "defiled the priesthood, and the covenant of the priesthood". In Hebrew, covenant and priesthood stand together only here and in Numbers 25:13.

@Malachi 2:4-6

Malachi speaks of a covenant "with Levi" of "life and peace", and of one who "did turn many away from iniquity". He does not name Phinehas. Covenant and peace stand together, and the verb turn is the one used when Phinehas "turned my wrath away". The link is in the words. Whether Malachi means Phinehas is left open.

## The other Phinehas

@1 Samuel 1:3

@1 Samuel 2:12

@1 Samuel 2:22

The second Phinehas, a son of Eli, served at Shiloh. 1 Samuel calls Eli's sons "sons of Belial" who "knew not the LORD". Verse 22 says they lay with the women who assembled "at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation". In Hebrew this is the same three-word phrase as Numbers 25:6, where the people wept and the first Phinehas rose up. The same name stands at the same door. One man rose against the sin there; the other committed it there.

@1 Samuel 2:30

@1 Samuel 2:34-35

@1 Samuel 3:12-14

The judgment on Eli's house is spoken in the words of Numbers 25. Phinehas son of Eleazar "made an atonement" and received an "everlasting" priesthood. Of Eli's house the LORD swears that its iniquity "shall not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever". Purged is the verb of atonement, and for ever is the word for everlasting. Where Numbers 25:11 says "I consumed not", 1 Samuel 3:12 says "when I begin, I will also make an end", with the same Hebrew verb.

@1 Samuel 4:4; 1 Samuel 4:10-11

@1 Samuel 4:17

The messenger's report uses the word that was "the plague" in Numbers 25. The "great slaughter" of verse 17 is the same Hebrew noun. In this verse the plague is not stayed, and Phinehas is dead.

@1 Samuel 4:19-21

His widow names her son I-chabod: "The glory is departed from Israel". The line goes on to Ahiah, a grandson of Phinehas, wearing an ephod at Shiloh in the days of Saul.

@1 Samuel 14:3

## Where the two lines meet

@1 Chronicles 24:3

David's priests came from two houses: "Zadok of the sons of Eleazar, and Ahimelech of the sons of Ithamar". By 1 Chronicles 6, Zadok's line runs through Phinehas son of Eleazar.

@1 Kings 2:26-27

@1 Kings 2:35

When Solomon removes Abiathar, the text says it was "that he might fulfil the word of the LORD, which he spake concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh". Then "Zadok the priest did the king put in the room of Abiathar". The priest put out belongs, by 1 Kings, to the house of Eli. The priest put in descends, by 1 Chronicles, from Phinehas son of Eleazar. 1 Samuel itself never names Eli's ancestor.

@Ezekiel 44:15

Ezekiel later names "the sons of Zadok" as the priests who "kept the charge of my sanctuary when the children of Israel went astray".

## Zeal in other hands

@1 Kings 19:10

@2 Kings 10:16

The same root comes back with Elijah, "very jealous for the LORD God of hosts", where the Hebrew doubles the verb, and with Jehu: "see my zeal for the LORD".

@Psalm 69:9

@John 2:17

@Romans 10:2

Phinehas is never named in the New Testament, but the word is. The disciples remembered Psalm 69:9 at the temple, and Paul speaks of "a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge". The Greek word behind zeal is rendered zeal six times and envying four times in the KJV, as the Hebrew covers both zeal and envy.

## What the text leaves open

The record leaves some things unexplained, and this study leaves them as questions. Each one is on the trail list in the workbench.

Did the judges ever carry out the order of Numbers 25:5? Numbers 25:9 counts twenty and four thousand, and Paul says three and twenty thousand fell "in one day". What did the covenant of Numbers 25 add to the everlasting priesthood already given in Exodus 40:15? Why did Moses send Phinehas to the war and not Eleazar? In Psalm 106:30, did Phinehas judge, or pray? How does the "for ever" taken from Eli's house read beside the "everlasting" promise to Phinehas? Is Malachi's covenant with Levi the covenant of Numbers 25?
